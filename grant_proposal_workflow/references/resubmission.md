# Resubmissions

A declined proposal comes with the most useful material a PI will ever get: the panel's own words about what kept it from being funded. Build the revision around them.

## 1. Gather the record

- The reviews, panel summary, and decision letter, transcribed into one text file (portals often deliver them as several PDFs or web pages). Keep the originals.
- The previous submission **as submitted** (the PDF assembled by the funder's system). It, not a later working draft, is the source of truth for "what did we say or budget last time".
- Anything the program officer said, informally or not, written down with the date. A remark about cost, scope, or fit with the program, however brief, can shape the whole revision.
- The previous cycle's signed letters, facilities text, and budget workbook, for comparison.
- Whether the funder has rules for resubmissions (an introduction responding to reviews, a limit on resubmissions, required marking of changes) and whether the call itself changed.

## 2. Two plans, kept separate

1. **Review-driven plan (strategy).** What the reviews say to change. Start with the decline reason quoted verbatim. Organize by tier:
   - Tier 1: the stated reasons for the decision. Non-negotiable.
   - Tier 2 onward: other weaknesses named by the panel summary, then by individual reviewers, roughly by how many raised them.
   - **Preserve:** strengths the panel praised. List them so revisions do not dilute them.
   - **Resolved by a scope cut:** critiques that disappear because the work was dropped. Do not answer these; delete the leftovers (see section 3).
   Each item names the review it comes from, the change, and where in the proposal it lands.
2. **Mechanical plan.** Placeholders, number consistency, formatting, page limits, documents to refresh. Useful but not what wins the revision.

Keep them in two files so the strategy is not buried under the mechanics.

## 3. Fixed page budget

If the panel or program officer said the proposal was too ambitious, treat the page count as fixed or shrinking: every expansion must be paid for by a cut. Prefer cutting a whole component over thinning everything. A cut that removes a component also removes the reviewer questions about it.

After any cut, search all documents for its leftovers and log them in the scope-change log:

- timeline rows and figure panels or labels (text inside image figures must be fixed in the source file)
- captions, "potential challenges" and risk paragraphs, assessment lines
- methods steps whose only purpose was the cut analysis, and sample sizes justified by it
- budget lines and spreadsheet labels, collaborators, letters, facilities paragraphs, mentoring-plan duties
- vision, summary, and introduction claims that motivated the cut work

Then re-justify what remains (e.g., a sample size originally argued from the cut analysis) and check whether the cut allows further budget savings.

## 4. One cycle later

- Shift the timeline: what was Year 2 is usually Year 1 now. Re-date every activity, conference, field season, and rate (salaries, tuition, per diem) to the new budget years.
- Confirm every collaborator is still at the same institution with the same title; people move, and a letter from someone who has left is an orphan.
- Refresh eligibility (career-stage windows, number of prior attempts allowed).
- Refresh institutional boilerplate (facilities, equipment, education programs) against the institution's current template instead of carrying last year's text.

## 5. Check that the revision answers the reviews

- Build a response table: critique → change made → location. If the funder allows or requires an introduction responding to the reviews, write it from this table, crediting reviewers where they were right and never sounding defensive.
- In the review round, run a separate prior-review compliance agent (see `review-panel.md`) and keep the blind panel unaware of the previous reviews. If blind panelists raise the same concerns that sank the last submission, those concerns are not yet fixed, whatever the response table says.
